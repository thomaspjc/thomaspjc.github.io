About

My research sits between experiment, simulation, and design. I build computational tools for structured laser shaping, study how those profiles influence electron beam formation, and work toward practical accelerator applications where beam quality and stability matter. More broadly, I am interested in accelerator science, beam physics, laser-based control, and the use of optimization and machine learning in physical systems.

Research

My current work centers on shaping ultraviolet laser profiles with nanofabricated phase plates to improve transverse emittance in electron sources. I am also expanding into new accelerator research directions involving beam physics and amplified optical stochastic cooling.

UV Phase Plates for Photoinjectors

University of Chicago · Argonne · SLAC

Designing and testing fused-silica phase plates that transform Gaussian laser beams into profiles better suited for photocathode operation, with the goal of improving beam uniformity and reducing emittance.

Computational Beam Propagation and Inverse Design

Simulation and optimization

Building Python workflows for Fresnel propagation, target matching, and structured phase retrieval to connect optical design choices with experimentally relevant beam profiles.

Transition Toward Beam Physics at IOTA

New direction

I am moving from the photoinjector and laser-shaping side of accelerator science toward broader beam physics questions, including work in Jonathan Jarvis’ group at IOTA on amplified optical stochastic cooling. This transition reflects my long-term goal of contributing across the full beamline, from source development to beam manipulation and control.
