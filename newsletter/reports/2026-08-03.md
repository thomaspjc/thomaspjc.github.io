## 1. High-Density Monocular 3D Particle Image Velocimetry by Wavefront Shaping and Deep Learning

**Authors:** Dafei Xiao, Jibu Tom Jose, Amit Parizat, Reut Orange Kedem, Josué Sznitman, Omri Ram, Yoav Shechtman

**Categories:** physics.optics, physics.flu-dyn

**Published:** July 31, 2026

**Relevance:** 2.0/10

**Confidence:** 0.90

**Suggested Project:** None

### Summary

The paper presents PET‑PIV, a compact monocular 3‑D particle image velocimetry technique that uses a thin phase mask to engineer the point‑spread function (PSF) and deep‑learning algorithms to recover 3‑D velocity fields from a single camera. Two operating modes are introduced: a tracking mode that localizes and links individual particles using a neural network (LocNet) and a field mode that directly predicts volumetric velocity fields from image sequences using a second network (VelNet). Experimental validation against a tomographic PIV system demonstrates high accuracy (correlation coefficients >0.97) and an order‑of‑magnitude speed improvement, enabling high‑density 3‑D velocimetry in space‑constrained environments.

### Key Contributions

- Introduction of PET‑PIV, a monocular 3‑D PIV framework combining PSF engineering with deep learning.
- Design and implementation of a thin phase mask (Tetrapod) to encode depth information in particle images.
- Development of two complementary neural network pipelines: LocNet for particle tracking and VelNet for field‑mode velocity reconstruction.
- Experimental validation against tomographic PIV showing >0.97 correlation and >10× faster processing.
- Demonstration of high‑density (up to 0.4 particles/mm³) 3‑D velocity measurement with a single camera.
- Provision of a scalable, low‑cost optical modification suitable for both microscopy and macro‑scale imaging.

## 2. Optimized Phase Masks for Absorption of Ultra-Broadband Pulses by Narrowband Atomic Ensembles

**Authors:** L. B. A. Mélo, Daniel Felinto, Marcio H. G. de Miranda

**Categories:** physics.atom-ph, physics.optics, quant-ph

**Published:** February 23, 2026

**Relevance:** 2.0/10

**Confidence:** 0.90

**Suggested Project:** None

### Summary

The paper presents a theoretical study of enhancing two‑photon cascade absorption in narrowband atomic ensembles by shaping the spectral phase of ultrafast pulses with a spatial light modulator (SLM) optimized via a genetic algorithm (GA). It compares single‑pulse and dual‑pulse excitation schemes, quantifies enhancement factors (up to 26× for two‑pulse configuration), and investigates the effect of atomic density and optical depth, including the formation of zero‑area pulses in dense media.

### Key Contributions

- Development of a GA‑based optimization framework for spectral phase masks to maximize two‑photon cascade absorption.
- Demonstration of significant absorption enhancement (≈9.5× for single‑pulse, ≈26× for dual‑pulse) compared to Fourier‑transform‑limited excitation.
- Analysis of the impact of atomic density and optical depth on the optimized absorption, showing modest gains under realistic experimental conditions.
- Investigation of zero‑area pulse formation in dense ensembles and its influence on two‑photon absorption efficiency.

## 3. Near-optimal decomposition of unitary matrices using phase masks and the discrete Fourier transform

**Authors:** Vincent Girouard, Nicolás Quesada

**Categories:** physics.optics, quant-ph

**Published:** August 27, 2025

**Relevance:** 2.0/10

**Confidence:** 0.90

**Suggested Project:** None

### Summary

The paper presents a constructive analytical decomposition of arbitrary unitary matrices into a sequence of phase masks and discrete Fourier transform (DFT) layers, requiring only $2N+5$ phase masks and $2N+4$ DFT matrices for an $N$‑mode system. This scheme offers a compact, loss‑robust design for universal multiport interferometers (UMIs) based on multimode interference couplers, reducing the optical depth by a factor of three compared to previous analytical approaches.

### Key Contributions

- Introduces a near‑optimal analytical decomposition of unitary matrices using $2N+5$ phase masks interleaved with $2N+4$ DFT matrices.
- Provides a constructive design method for UMIs that are robust to losses and fabrication errors.
- Reduces the optical depth of analytical UMI designs by a factor of three relative to earlier methods.
- Enables implementation of universal interferometers using phase masks and multimode interference couplers.
- Offers an alternative to numerical optimization for designing high‑performance linear optical networks.

### Why It Matters

The paper focuses on optical interferometer design and unitary matrix decomposition, which are not directly related to accelerator physics topics such as beam dynamics, stochastic cooling, or photoinjector optimization. While phase masks and Fourier transforms are optical tools, the work does not address accelerator‑specific applications or methods, making it only marginally relevant.

## 4. Phase mask fabrication for multi-plane light conversion using grayscale lithography

**Authors:** Sudip Gurung, Seth Smith Dryden, Keqi Qin, Guifang Li

**Categories:** physics.optics, physics.app-ph

**Published:** July 14, 2025

**Relevance:** 2.0/10

**Confidence:** 0.90

**Suggested Project:** None

### Summary

The paper presents a direct‑writing laser grayscale lithography process for fabricating multi‑plane light conversion (MPLC) phase masks. The technique transfers precise depth profiles onto substrates via reactive ion etching followed by reflective coating, achieving sub‑10 nm vertical resolution and <3 nm surface roughness. An MPLC system using these masks converts a Gaussian TEM(0,0) input to a Laguerre‑Gaussian LG(0,0) mode with 92 % fidelity and an R² of 0.976, demonstrating a scalable alternative to conventional multi‑step lithography for advanced photonic systems.

### Key Contributions

- Introduction of DWL grayscale lithography for direct fabrication of MPLC phase masks
- Demonstration of sub‑10 nm vertical resolution and <3 nm surface roughness in fabricated masks
- Achieving 92 % mode‑conversion fidelity from Gaussian to LG(0,0) with high R² (0.976)
- Providing a scalable fabrication alternative to multi‑step lithographic processes

### Why It Matters

**Relevance:** 2.0/10
