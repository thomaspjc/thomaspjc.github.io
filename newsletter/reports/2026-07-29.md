## 1. Realizing Steady-State Microbunching with Optical Stochastic Crystallization

**Authors:** M. Wallbank, J. Jarvis

**Categories:** physics.acc-ph

**Published:** September 10, 2024

**Relevance:** 9.0/10

**Confidence:** 0.90

**Suggested Project:** Amplified Stochastic Oscillator for the IOTA Ring

### Summary

The paper reports on the status and future plans of the optical stochastic cooling (OSC) program at Fermilab’s IOTA ring, emphasizing the use of an optical amplifier to achieve higher cooling rates and the novel concept of Optical Stochastic Crystallization (OSX) for steady‑state microbunching (SSMB). It outlines the OSC mechanism, the requirements for OSX, preliminary simulations, and the upcoming experimental run in 2025 aimed at demonstrating SSMB.

### Key Contributions

- Demonstration of the OSC cooling mechanism and its extension to a high‑gain optical amplifier configuration.
- Introduction and theoretical description of Optical Stochastic Crystallization (OSX) as a pathway to steady‑state microbunching.
- Identification of key lattice and optical parameters (gain, momentum compaction, slip factor sign, dispersion invariant) necessary for OSX.
- Preliminary 1D and 3D simulations showing microbunch formation and self‑reinforcing energy kicks.
- Plan for an experimental demonstration of SSMB at IOTA in 2025, including integration of reinforcement‑learning control strategies.
- Experimental verification of low‑alpha operation and high‑gain amplifier performance at the FAST laser lab.
- Discussion of collective effects (CSR, IBS) and their impact on future experiments.

## 2. Experimental demonstration of OSC at IOTA: IOTA Run #3 Report

**Authors:** J. Jarvis, V. Lebedev, A. Romanov, D. Broemmelsiek, K. Carlson, S. Chattopadhyay, A. Dick, D. Edstrom, I. Lobach, S. Nagaitsev, H. Piekarz, P. Piot, J. Ruan, J. Santucci, G. Stancari, A. Valishev

**Categories:** physics.acc-ph

**Published:** April 11, 2022

**Relevance:** 9.5/10

**Confidence:** 0.90

**Suggested Project:** Amplified Stochastic Oscillator for the IOTA Ring

### Summary

The paper reports the first experimental demonstration of optical stochastic cooling (OSC) in the IOTA storage ring. Run #3 of IOTA, conducted from November 2020 to August 2021, installed and commissioned an OSC apparatus comprising wigglers, optical amplifiers, and a dedicated optical transport line. The experiment achieved strong cooling in one, two, and three dimensions, validated theoretical OSC models, and demonstrated OSC with a single electron. Key diagnostics included undulator radiation imaging, a streak camera for longitudinal distribution, and a SPAD for fast timing. The study confirmed OSC’s ability to modify beam phase‑space distributions and provided systematic measurements of cooling and heating regimes.

### Key Contributions

- First experimental validation of OSC in a storage ring
- Demonstration of multi‑dimensional cooling (longitudinal, transverse, 3‑D)
- Validation of OSC theory through systematic measurements
- Implementation of single‑electron OSC experiments
- Development of integrated diagnostics (UR imaging, streak camera, SPAD) for OSC characterization

## 3. Photonic Structures Optimization Using Highly Data-Efficient Deep Learning: Application To Nanofin And Annular Groove Phase Masks

**Authors:** Nicolas Roy, Lorenzo König, Olivier Absil, Charlotte Beauthier, Alexandre Mayer, Michaël Lobet

**Categories:** physics.optics, cond-mat.mes-hall, cs.AI, physics.space-ph

**Published:** September 5, 2023

**Relevance:** 2.0/10

**Confidence:** 0.90

**Suggested Project:** None

### Summary

The paper presents a data‑efficient surrogate optimization framework for designing thin‑film photonic devices, specifically vortex phase masks for high‑contrast astronomical imaging. It demonstrates that conventional surrogate models (partial least squares, Kriging, radial basis functions) are inadequate for these devices and proposes a deep neural network trained on a limited set of simulations as a highly accurate surrogate. A robust particle‑swarm evolutionary optimizer then searches the explicit geometric parameter space, achieving optimal designs while reducing the required number of full electromagnetic simulations by up to 75%.

### Key Contributions

- Introduction of a deep‑neural‑network surrogate model for metasurface design that outperforms traditional surrogate methods.
- Application of a particle‑swarm evolutionary optimization scheme to the explicit geometric parameters of vortex phase masks.
- Demonstration that the combined surrogate–evolutionary approach can optimize complex photonic devices with a 75% reduction in simulation effort.
- Provision of two optimized vortex phase mask designs suitable for astronomical high‑contrast imaging.
- Analysis of the limitations of conventional surrogate models for this class of photonic devices.

### Why It Matters

The paper focuses on metasurface design and surrogate optimization for optical devices, which is unrelated to accelerator physics, beam dynamics, or machine learning applications in accelerators. Therefore it has only a very weak relevance to the user’s research profile.

## 4. Self-stimulated Emission of Undulator Radiation

**Authors:** E. G. Bessonov, M. V. Gorbunkov, A. A. Mikhailichenko, A. L. Osipov

**Categories:** physics.acc-ph, physics.class-ph

**Published:** March 19, 2010

**Relevance:** 8.0/10

**Confidence:** 0.90

**Suggested Project:** Amplified Stochastic Oscillator for the IOTA Ring

### Summary

The paper discusses the interaction between a charged particle in a downstream undulator and the wavelet it emitted in an upstream undulator, showing that this self‑stimulated emission can be as strong as the frictional field within the undulator itself. This effect can be exploited to enhance the signal from pickup undulators in optical stochastic cooling schemes and to increase damping.

### Key Contributions

- Identifies self‑stimulated emission of undulator radiation as a significant interaction mechanism.
- Demonstrates that the interaction strength can match that of the intrinsic undulator frictional field.
- Suggests practical applications for improving pickup signal strength and damping in optical stochastic cooling systems.

### Why It Matters

The paper directly addresses a mechanism that can enhance pickup undulator signals and damping in optical stochastic cooling, which is central to the Amplified Stochastic Oscillator project’s goals of understanding pickup‑to‑kicker radiation transport and cooling decrements.

## 5. Generation and measurement of sub-picosecond electron bunch in photocathode rf gun

**Authors:** Weiwei Li, Zhiagng He, Qika Jia

**Categories:** physics.acc-ph

**Published:** May 21, 2013

**Relevance:** 8.0/10

**Confidence:** 0.90

**Suggested Project:** Machine Learning for Photoinjector Beam Quality Enhancement

### Summary

The paper presents a method to produce sub‑picosecond electron bunches in a photocathode RF gun by increasing the gun’s acceleration gradient, optimizing bunch charge, laser spot size, and acceleration phase, and reducing transverse emittance growth through laser shaping. It also introduces a nondestructive diagnostic that measures bunch length via the high‑frequency spectrum of wakefield radiation emitted as the bunch traverses a dielectric‑lined channel.

### Key Contributions

- Improved acceleration gradient in the RF gun to achieve sub‑picosecond bunches
- Optimization of bunch charge, laser spot size, and acceleration phase for minimal emittance growth
- Laser shaping technique to reduce transverse emittance
- Nondestructive bunch‑length measurement using wakefield radiation spectrum

### Why It Matters

The paper directly addresses photocathode RF gun operation and beam quality, which aligns closely with the goals of the Machine Learning for Photoinjector Beam Quality Enhancement project. It provides technical insights that could inform machine‑learning models for injector optimization, making it highly relevant to the user’s research profile.

## 6. Mapping charge capture and acceleration in a plasma wakefield of a proton bunch using variable emittance electron beam injection

**Authors:** E. Granados, L. Verra, A. -M. Bachmann, E. Chevallay, S. Doebert, V. Fedosseev, F. Friebel, S. Gessner, E. Gschwendtner, S. Y. Kim, S. Mazzoni, J. T. Moody, M. Turner

**Categories:** physics.acc-ph, physics.plasm-ph

**Published:** June 28, 2022

**Relevance:** 2.0/10

**Confidence:** 0.90

**Suggested Project:** None

### Summary

The study reports on the charge capture and acceleration of electron bunches in a proton‑driven plasma wakefield experiment (AWAKE Phase 2). Using an RF‑gun with a Cs₂Te photocathode illuminated by a tunable UV laser, the authors produced electron bunches with variable charge, emittance, and injection trajectory. By adjusting the UV beam optics and timing relative to the ionizing laser, they achieved charge capture rates exceeding 15 % (40 pC accelerated from a 385 pC injected bunch) under optimized conditions.

### Key Contributions

- Demonstrated variable‑emittance electron injection into a proton‑driven plasma wakefield using a tunable UV‑laser‑illuminated RF‑gun.
- Quantified charge capture efficiency (>15 %) and shot‑to‑shot reproducibility for different injection parameters.
- Provided a flexible optical system for controlling electron bunch size, duration, position, and timing relative to the plasma seed laser.
- Showed that optimized injection yields significant accelerated charge (40 pC) from a 385 pC injected bunch.

### Why It Matters

The paper focuses on plasma wakefield acceleration and electron beam injection, which is not directly related to the user’s current projects on optical stochastic cooling, reinforcement learning for beam dynamics, or machine learning for photoinjector optimization. Therefore its relevance to the stated research profile is minimal.
