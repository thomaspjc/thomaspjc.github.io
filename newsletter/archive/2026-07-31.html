1. Improved companion mass limits for Sirius A with thermal infrared coronagraphy using a vector-apodizing phase plate and time-domain starlight-subtraction techniques

Authors: Joseph D. Long, Jared R. Males, Sebastiaan Y. Haffert, Logan Pearce, Mark S. Marley, Katie M. Morzinski, Laird M. Close, Gilles P. P. L. Otten, Frans Snik, Matthew A. Kenworthy, Christoph U. Keller, Philip Hinz, John D. Monnier, Alycia Weinberger, Volker Tolls

Categories: astro-ph.EP, astro-ph.IM

Published: March 9, 2023

Relevance: 1.0/10

Confidence: 0.90

Suggested Project: None

Summary

The study presents high-contrast imaging of Sirius A at 3.9 µm using the MagAO/Clio system with a grating vector‑apodizing phase plate (gvAPP‑180) coronagraph. A novel time‑domain principal‑components‑analysis algorithm (PCA‑Temporal, PCAT) is introduced to subtract residual starlight by operating on eigen‑time‑series, reducing computational cost and enabling use of the full dataset. The authors achieve post‑processed contrasts of 1.5–9.8×10⁻⁶ beyond 0.75″, corresponding to planet masses of 2.6–8.0 M_J, and combine these limits with previous studies to rule out 99 % of ≥9 M_J companions between 2.5–7 AU.

Key Contributions

  • Implementation of a gvAPP‑180 coronagraph in thermal infrared imaging of a bright star
  • Development of the PCA‑Temporal (PCAT) algorithm for efficient starlight subtraction using eigen‑time‑series
  • Demonstration of improved contrast limits at small angular separations via full‑frame temporal analysis
  • Combination of new limits with literature to produce a completeness analysis excluding massive companions around Sirius A

2. Upgrading LBTI/NOMIC with a quadruple annular groove phase mask and GeoSnap detector for imaging nearby, habitable-zone exoplanets

Authors: Kevin Wagner, Manny Montoya, Steve Ertel, Jarron Leisenring, Pontus Forsberg, Samuel Ronayette, Andre Wong, Mikael Karlsson, Olivier Absil, Denis Defrère, Markus Kasper, Jordan Stone, Dániel Apai, Laird Close, Jamie Dietrich, Ewan Douglas, Jamie Drew, Olivier Durney, Marina Fetisova, Kyran Grattan, Olivier Guyon, Jacob Isbell, Sebastián Jorquera, Petri Karvinen, Markku Kuittinen, Hervé Le Coroller, Jared Males, Brittany Miles, Dillon O'Reilly, Eric Pantin, Sascha P. Categories: astro-ph.IM, astro-ph.EP

Published: July 30, 2026

Relevance: 1.0/10

Confidence: 0.90

Suggested Project: None

Summary

The paper reports two in‑progress upgrades to the Large Binocular Telescope Interferometer’s Nulling‑Optimized Mid‑Infrared Camera (NOMIC): a quadruple annular groove phase mask (Q‑AGPM) coronagraph optimized for the N‑band (≈11 µm) and a 13 µm‑cutoff Teledyne GeoSnap HgCdTe detector. The Q‑AGPM places four vortex masks on a single diamond substrate, enabling dual‑aperture chopping without loss of efficiency and achieving on‑axis rejection ratios of 10³–10⁴ across the science band. The GeoSnap array replaces the legacy AQUARIUS array, providing higher quantum efficiency, larger well depth, faster linear readout, and elimination of excess low‑frequency noise, thereby improving contrast and sensitivity at small angular separations. The paper also presents on‑sky Fizeau imaging results with a new FFTCam fringe tracker, demonstrating a 2–4× contrast gain over single‑aperture exposures and confirming the interferometric advantage for resolving habitable‑zone planets around nearby Sun‑like stars.

Key Contributions

  • Design, fabrication, and installation of a quadruple annular groove phase mask coronagraph optimized for N‑band imaging.
  • Replacement of the AQUARIUS detector with a 13 µm‑cutoff Teledyne GeoSnap array, improving QE, well depth, and readout linearity.
  • Demonstration of on‑sky Fizeau imaging with a new FFTCam fringe tracker, achieving 2–4× contrast gain over single‑aperture mode.
  • Quantification of the expected contrast and sensitivity improvements for imaging habitable‑zone exoplanets with the upgraded GNOMIC instrument.

Two effects should make the N-band gains stronger still. First, fringe stability improves at longer wavelengths — i.e., optical-path-difference errors are a fixed physical length, whereas the residual phase error scales as a fraction of the wavelength. This especially enhances the gain in the contrast- limited regions close to the star. Second, the background-limited regime is reached at smaller separations (in units of λ/D), so the larger background-limited gain takes over closer to the star, though not in absolute angle given the coarser resolution at N band. 3. Resonant RF Wakefield Coupling for Radiation-Reaction Control of 3D Betatron Dynamics in Hybrid Laser Plasma Accelerators

Authors: A. A. Molavi Choobini, M. Shahmansouri

Categories: physics.acc-ph, physics.comp-ph, physics.plasm-ph

Published: April 27, 2026

Relevance: 3.0/10

Confidence: 0.80

Suggested Project: None

Summary

The paper presents a combined analytical and 3D PIC study of ultra‑relativistic electron bunch dynamics in hybrid laser‑plasma RF accelerators. It shows that externally applied RF fields can act as a tunable lattice, enabling precise control of betatron oscillation amplitude, polarization, and transverse focusing gradients. By resonantly matching RF frequencies to natural betatron frequencies, the authors demonstrate enhanced transverse excursions while increasing radiative damping via classical radiation reaction (Landau–Lifshitz) and quantum synchrotron‑like losses, leading to emittance reduction and improved beam stability.

Key Contributions

  • Analytical model of spatiotemporal plasma wakefield modulation combined with phase‑dependent RF‑driven oscillations.
  • 3D PIC simulations incorporating classical radiation reaction and quantum synchrotron loss effects.
  • Demonstration of a resonant RF–betatron alignment regime that enhances controlled transverse motion and increases radiative damping.
  • Explicit stability maps and force landscapes showing time‑dependent gamma‑factor growth rates linked to longitudinal field gradients and injection conditions.
  • Proof that external RF fields can serve as a tunable lattice for deterministic regulation of transverse focusing and betatron amplitudes, yielding ultra‑stable, high‑quality electron beams with desired polarization states.

Why It Matters
